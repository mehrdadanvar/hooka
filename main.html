Study Design and Population

This descriptive epidemiological study employed a cross-sectional design to investigate the patterns of cigarette and hookah smoking within the population of Southern Iran. Data were collected using a combination of in-person surveys and an online questionnaire distribution strategy. The target population included adult residents of Southern Iran.

Data Collection

A structured questionnaire was developed to collect data on smoking behaviors, knowledge, and attitudes. The questionnaire comprised seven distinct sections:

  • Section 1: Background and Demographics: This section collected a range of demographic information, including age (in years), gender (identifying as male, female, or other), nationality, highest level of educational attainment (e.g., high school, college degree, postgraduate), current occupation,province/territory of residence.
  • Section 2: Smoking Knowledge, Attitudes, and Status: Eleven items assessed participants' understanding of and opinions regarding both cigarette and hookah smoking. Current smoking status for each product (daily, occasional, former, never) was also determined.
  • Section 3: Cigarette Smoking Patterns and Behavior: For participants reporting current cigarette use, detailed questions explored their smoking history and habits. This included age at smoking initiation (in years), primary motivations for smoking (multiple choice with an "other" option), typical daily cigarette consumption (number of cigarettes), indicators of nicotine dependence (e.g., time to first cigarette, difficulty refraining), common smoking contexts (e.g., with meals, socializing), and perceptions of cigarette smoking and related social norms (using Likert scales).
  • Section 4: Attitude Towards Quitting Cigarette Smoking: This section focused on current cigarette smokers' perspectives on quitting. It assessed their desire to quit (Likert scale), history of quit attempts (yes/no, number of attempts), reasons for past quit attempts, duration of previous attempts (in days/weeks/months), factors contributing to relapse, methods used for cessation (e.g., nicotine replacement therapy, counseling), experienced withdrawal symptoms (checklist), self-efficacy in quitting and perceived social support for quitting.
  • Section 5: Hookah Smoking Patterns and Behavior: Participants who reported current hookah use provided information on their smoking history and habits, including age at initiation (in years), reasons for smoking hookah (multiple choice with an "other" option), frequency of use (e.g., times per week/month), typical duration of a smoking session (in minutes), types of tobacco/flavorings typically used, perceived dependence on hookah (Likert scale), common situations of use (e.g., social gatherings, relaxation), factors influencing initiation and attitudes towards hookah smoking and its social acceptability (using Likert scales).
  • Section 6: Attitude Towards Quitting Hookah Smoking: This section explored current hookah smokers' intentions and experiences with quitting. Questions addressed their desire to quit (Likert scale), history of quit attempts (yes/no, number of attempts), reasons for attempting to quit, duration of previous attempts (in days/weeks/months), factors leading to relapse, cessation methods employed (e.g., reducing frequency, seeking support), experienced withdrawal symptoms (checklist), confidence in successfully quitting, and perceived social support for quitting hookah (Likert scale).
  • Section 7: Respiratory Health: Participants were asked about their recent respiratory experiences, including the frequency of shortness of breath in the past month (e.g., never, sometimes, often), presence and nature of a cough (productive/non-productive), perceived impact of respiratory symptoms on their physical activity levels (Likert scale), and any prior medical diagnoses of respiratory illnesses (yes/no, with an open-ended option to specify the condition(s)).

Questionnaire Development

The questionnaire was developed following a review of relevant literature and existing validated instruments, with modifications made to ensure cultural appropriateness and relevance to the study population. The questionnaire was piloted to assess clarity, comprehensiveness, and face validity prior to data collection.

Survey Administration

Data collection was conducted through two primary methods:

  1. In-Person Surveys: Trained surveyors administered the questionnaire to participants in various community settings within Southern Iran. Surveyors received standardized training to ensure consistency in data collection procedures.

Data Analysis

Descriptive statistical methods were used to analyze the collected data. Frequencies and percentages were empployed to calculated to summarize the demographic characteristics of the study population and the prevalence of cigarette and hookah smoking. Measures of central tendency (mean, median) and dispersion (standard deviation) will be used to describe continuous variables, such as age. Knowledge and attitude scores will be calculated and analyzed descriptively. Data will be analyzed to identify patterns of cigarette and hookah smoking across different demographic subgroups. Statistical software (e.g., SPSS) will be used for data management and analysis.
