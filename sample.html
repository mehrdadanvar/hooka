Study Design and Population

This descriptive epidemiological study employed a cross-sectional design to investigate the patterns of cigarette and hookah smoking within the population of Southern Iran. Data were collected using a combination of in-person surveys and an online questionnaire distribution strategy. The target population included adult residents of Southern Iran.

Data Collection

A structured questionnaire was developed to collect data on smoking behaviors, knowledge, and attitudes. The questionnaire comprised seven distinct sections:

  • Section 1: Background and Demographics: This section gathered data on participant characteristics, including age, gender, nationality, education level, occupation, province of residence, and marital status.
  • Section 2: Smoking Knowledge, Attitudes, and Status: This section assessed participants' knowledge and attitudes towards cigarette and hookah smoking through a series of 11 questions. Current smoking status (for both cigarettes and hookah) was also determined in this section.
  • Section 3: Cigarette Smoking Patterns and Behavior: This section aimed to comprehensively assess the characteristics and habits of participants who reported current cigarette smoking. It explored various aspects of their smoking behavior, including the age of initiation, reasons for smoking, patterns of daily consumption, nicotine dependence indicators, circumstances surrounding smoking, and attitudes towards smoking and related social norms.
  • Section 4: Attitude Towards Quitting Cigarette Smoking: This section focused on understanding the perspectives and experiences of current cigarette smokers regarding cessation. It investigated their desire to quit, any prior quit attempts, the reasons for attempting to quit, the number and duration of past attempts, reasons for relapse, methods used to quit, experienced withdrawal symptoms, self-efficacy in quitting, and perceived social support for quitting.
  • Section 5: Hookah Smoking Patterns and Behavior: This section aimed to detail the smoking habits of participants who reported current hookah use. It gathered information on their age of initiation, reasons for smoking hookah, frequency and patterns of use, time spent per session, types of tobacco used, perceived dependence, circumstances of use, initiation factors, and attitudes towards hookah smoking and related social perceptions.
  • Section 6: Attitude Towards Quitting Hookah Smoking: This section explored the desire and experiences of current hookah smokers regarding cessation. It investigated their intention to quit, history of quit attempts, reasons for attempting to quit, the number and duration of past attempts, reasons for relapse, methods employed for quitting, experienced withdrawal symptoms, their confidence in quitting successfully, and perceived social support for quitting hookah.
  • Section 7: Respiratory Health: This section aimed to gather information on participants' recent respiratory symptoms and history. It included questions about experiencing shortness of breath in the past month, the presence of a productive cough, perceived impact on physical ability, and any prior diagnoses of respiratory illnesses, as well as the specific conditions diagnosed.

Questionnaire Development

The questionnaire was developed following a review of relevant literature and existing validated instruments, with modifications made to ensure cultural appropriateness and relevance to the study population. The questionnaire was piloted to assess clarity, comprehensiveness, and face validity prior to data collection.

Survey Administration

Data collection was conducted through two primary methods:

  1. In-Person Surveys: Trained surveyors administered the questionnaire to participants in various community settings within Southern Iran. Surveyors received standardized training to ensure consistency in data collection procedures.

Data Analysis

Descriptive statistical methods were used to analyze the collected data. Frequencies and percentages were empployed to calculated to summarize the demographic characteristics of the study population and the prevalence of cigarette and hookah smoking. Measures of central tendency (mean, median) and dispersion (standard deviation) will be used to describe continuous variables, such as age. Knowledge and attitude scores will be calculated and analyzed descriptively. Data will be analyzed to identify patterns of cigarette and hookah smoking across different demographic subgroups. Statistical software (e.g., SPSS) will be used for data management and analysis.
